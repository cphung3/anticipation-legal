Privacy policy

ANTicipation — countdown to joy · Last updated 3 November 2026

This policy explains what stays on your device, what can be stored in Firebase, and what information optional services receive.

The short version

Your countdowns are saved on your device first. You do not need to sign in to use the app. When Firebase is configured and available, the app creates an anonymous account identifier and can sync countdowns and moods to Firebase. Sign in with Apple or Google to link an account and restore your cloud data on another device. Photos you attach stay in app storage and are not uploaded.

What we keep, and where

On your device. Countdown details, moods, notes and ratings, preferences, and attached photos are stored locally. Photos are copied into the app's documents directory. Their local file paths are not uploaded or synced.

In Firebase, when available. Countdown titles, dates, categories, notes, ratings, and daily moods can sync under your Firebase account identifier. When Firebase is configured, an anonymous identifier is created on first launch; it is not linked to your name or email unless you choose to sign in.

If you sign in with Apple or Google. Firebase receives your email address and, if supplied by the provider, your display name. These identify your account for backup and restore. We do not email you.

Optional movie and TV artwork

In releases where the artwork service is configured, the movie or show title you search for is sent to our Firebase service and then to TMDB to find a poster. Loading a poster contacts TMDB's image servers. The service verifies your Firebase account and an app attestation token. For rate limiting it stores a hashed account identifier and a short-lived request count; it caches artwork by a hashed search key rather than storing the raw title. Rate-limit records are scheduled to expire after two minutes, and cached artwork after up to 30 days. Expired records are removed asynchronously. The service is optional; countdowns continue to work without it or an internet connection.

This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

Suggested releases in Discover

When contextual discovery is enabled, Discover can refresh a shared list of upcoming films, games, and show premieres from its configured catalog service, at most daily. Your region and game-platform choices filter that list on your device. The request sends no account token, saved countdowns, or interests. The hosting service receives ordinary connection information such as your IP address and user agent; we do not use it to build an advertising profile. Turning contextual discovery off stops new refreshes.

Our server fetches public release calendars from TVmaze and Wikipedia without sending your preferences. Source and data-license links appear in each release's details. A saved release can keep its public catalog identifier with your countdown, including in your cloud backup, to avoid suggesting it twice. Bundled and cached suggestions remain usable offline.

Searching the web for images

The in-app Search the web browser opens Google Images with your search query. Google and any website you visit receive the requests needed to load their pages and ordinary connection information, such as your IP address and browser details. The embedded browser may also send cookies it has for those sites, subject to their settings and policies.

If you choose an image, the app separately fetches its public HTTPS image URL to check and preview it. That request does not use the browser's cookies or page credentials, and the image host receives ordinary connection information. If you save a remote image as countdown art, its URL is saved with the countdown and may sync to Firebase or be included when you share that countdown. The app does not upload the image bytes. Images you attach from your device are copied into app storage; their local file paths are not synced.

Sharing a countdown

When you share a countdown, its title, category, target date, hype text, and remote artwork URL (if present) are stored in a shared Firebase record. Members can see those details, along with your display name and latest mood emoji if you have logged one. A local photo path is not included.

Anyone who has the six-character invite code can join, up to twelve members. There is no public feed, user search, comments, or messaging. Leaving removes your member profile from the shared countdown.

Crash reports

Release builds send crash reports and caught errors to Firebase Crashlytics so we can diagnose problems. Reports can include stack traces, error details, app and device information, and Firebase/Crashlytics installation identifiers used to group reports and count affected installations. We do not use those identifiers for advertising. See Firebase's Crashlytics privacy information.

What we never do

We do not collect your location or read your contacts. The app has no ads, advertising SDK, or analytics SDK. We do not sell or rent your data or share it with data brokers. Crash reporting and the optional services described above do send the information needed to provide those features.

Deleting your data

Two things live in Settings, on the You screen, and both do exactly what they say:

  • Delete all my data removes cloud countdowns, moods, owned shared countdowns, and memberships, then clears countdowns, moods, preferences, app-managed photos, and pending notifications on the device.
  • Delete my account performs the cloud cleanup, deletes the Firebase account, and then clears this device.

With an active Firebase session, cloud cleanup must succeed before the app clears local data. If cloud cleanup fails, the app reports the failure and keeps local data so you can retry. Without an active Firebase session, Delete all my data clears the device copy but cannot check for or remove a cloud copy from an earlier session; that copy may remain. Account deletion requests can also be made using the instructions on the account-deletion page. Firebase and store-provider operational logs may remain for the limited period needed for security, fraud prevention, or legal compliance; they are not used to restore your account.

Uninstalling removes data stored on the device but does not delete a Firebase cloud copy. Delete your data in the app first if you want that copy removed.

Children

ANTicipation is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Where the data lives

Cloud data is stored with Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore, and Crashlytics), which may process it on servers outside your country. Data is encrypted in transit.

Changes

We will update this page when our data practices change. The update date appears above.
